【Generation Goal】
Generate a live-action vertigo shot in one take, the kind that ends a trailer: Alex stands on a window ledge high on a skyscraper, steps off, falls at real speed down the face of the tower while the camera dives after him and rolls through a half turn, then time stretches into slow motion on an upside-down view of the city with Alex small in the middle of it, and the camera slowly pushes in on his face.

【Camera and Event Script】
One continuous shot with no cuts. The first frames are a held full-body shot of Alex on the ledge from about five metres away, front-left, on a 28 mm lens, the city blurred behind him.
0 to 1 second, real speed: Alex stands on the ledge with the wind pulling at his jacket, looking out over the city, chest heaving, a trembling steadying breath. His face is white with fear and fixed with resolve, eyes wide, jaw tight, not a hint of a smile.
1 to 1.6 seconds: he crouches, the glass edge cracking under his toes, and pushes off outward into the air. The glass lip shatters into a spray of shards that falls with him.
1.6 to 4.9 seconds, real speed, the fall: he drops head-first and then spread-eagled, belly toward the street, the sleeves and hood of his jacket whipping and snapping, hair flattened back, the skin of his face rippling in the wind, eyes streaming and wide with raw terror and exhilaration, mouth stretched open in a silent gasp. The camera dives after him: it climbs, looks steeply down at the street far below and the towers converging, then plunges down the face of the building in a fast, smooth, curving move, swinging round to his side and rolling a half turn as it catches him. Glass shards tumble with him. The facade streaks past with heavy motion blur, the street rushing up.
4.9 to 5.6 seconds: time eases down into extreme slow motion, about one thirtieth of real speed, and stays there to the end. By 5.6 seconds the camera has finished rolling and is level beside him, and the view is upside-down: the city hangs from the top of the frame, towers reaching down toward the sky, which fills the bottom of the picture.
5.6 to 7.1 seconds, slow motion, the famous frame: Alex is small and dead centre, about a fifth of the frame height, falling in the middle of the upside-down city, the towers hanging full height at both edges, the glass shards drifting around him like glitter. His body is quiet and weightless now, arms open, hair and jacket floating.
7.1 to 11.6 seconds: the camera glides in toward him in one long soft push, still upside down and still level, arcing round to his front, the lens lengthening from wide toward 35 mm. His face grows in frame: eyes wide and wet, pupils huge, lips parted, a slow shaky breath, fear melting into awe, the pure suspended instant between terror and wonder.
11.6 to 13.1 seconds: a held close-up of his head and shoulders, the camera drifting only with the fall, his eyes looking straight into the lens, a single tear lifting from his cheek, the upside-down towers and open sky wheeling slowly behind his head.
Type of shot: a vertigo free-fall shot with a camera dive, a half roll and a slow-motion push-in, in the tradition of the great falling shots. Motion and blur: during the fall the facade, the street and the glass all streak with heavy, directional motion blur and the picture shakes with speed; at the upside-down frame the blur vanishes and everything is crisp, every pane of glass and every shard sharp. Lens and focus: a 28 mm wide lens for the fall and ledge, lengthening to about 35 mm as it pushes in, deep focus on the towers until the close-up, where only his face is sharp and the city behind softens into glowing bokeh. Light: hard golden sun and deep blue shade slicing across the towers, his face lit gold on one side and cool blue on the other, sun flares in the glass, a teal-and-orange cinema grade with rich contrast. Mood and feel: dizzying, breathless and awestruck, terror flipping into serene wonder. Emotion: raw fear and exhilaration, never a smile, ending in a still, wide-eyed awe. It must look like a frame from a big-budget thriller shot on film, not a video game, not CGI and not a render.
Every action is performed with natural, realistic human motion: real weight and balance, momentum carried through each movement, and loose, believable arms and shoulders. The written description of the action decides how it looks.
Every person in the video is a real human being with natural skin, hair and clothing. No mannequin, clay figure, capsule, faceless figure or doll appears anywhere, at any moment.
Characters keep their mouths naturally closed; there is no narration, and no subtitles or on-screen text appear.
The sound includes only the roar of wind, the crack and tinkle of glass, a pounding heartbeat and his ragged breath, all dropping low and stretched when the footage slows, and a deep sub-bass hum under the slow motion. There is no speech and there is no music of any kind: no score, no song, no singing and no humming.
The final visuals are live-action and photographic: a real financial district of glass-and-steel skyscrapers photographed at magic hour: a low gold-orange sun raking across the towers and throwing long hard shadows down the avenues, glittering glass facades reflecting the sky, a thin amber haze, wispy high cloud lit pink and violet, real street-level traffic, trees and tiny cars far below. The image is photorealistic and cinematic: a large-format digital cinema camera, natural film grain, shallow depth of field where the lens allows, real atmospheric haze and depth, motivated light with soft falloff and rich but natural contrast, and real textured materials everywhere.
