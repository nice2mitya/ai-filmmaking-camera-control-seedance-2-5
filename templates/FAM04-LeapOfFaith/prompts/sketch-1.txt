【Camera and Event Script】
One continuous shot with no cuts. The first frames are a held full-body shot of Alex on the ledge from about five metres away, front-left, on a 28 mm lens, the city blurred behind him.
0 to 1 second, real speed: Alex stands on the ledge with the wind pulling at his jacket, looking out over the city, chest heaving, a trembling steadying breath. His face is calm and certain: steady eyes, a level gaze out over the city, jaw set, shoulders loose, the face of a man who has already decided and is in control. He is confident, not frightened, and not theatrical.
1 to 1.6 seconds: he crouches on the solid ledge and pushes off outward into the air. Nothing breaks: the ledge, the windows and the glass stay perfectly intact, and no glass, debris or shard ever falls.
1.6 to 4.9 seconds, real speed, the fall: he drops head-first and then spread-eagled, belly toward the street, the sleeves and hood of his jacket whipping and snapping, hair flattened back, the skin of his face rippling in the wind, eyes clear, open and fixed ahead, jaw set, a controlled breath, composed and exhilarated, a man who chose to jump and is enjoying the fall, never a scream, never panic. The camera dives after him: it climbs, looks steeply down at the street far below and the towers converging, then plunges down the face of the building in a fast, smooth, curving move, swinging round to his side and rolling a half turn as it catches him. The facade streaks past with heavy motion blur, the street rushing up.
4.9 to 5.6 seconds: time eases down into extreme slow motion, about one thirtieth of real speed, and stays there to the end. By 5.6 seconds the camera has finished rolling and is level beside him, and the view is upside-down: the city hangs from the top of the frame, towers reaching down toward the sky, which fills the bottom of the picture.
5.6 to 7.1 seconds, slow motion, the famous frame: Alex is small and dead centre, about a fifth of the frame height, falling in the middle of the upside-down city, the towers hanging full height at both edges, nothing drifts around him but air and light. His body is quiet and weightless now, arms open, hair and jacket floating.
7.1 to 11.6 seconds: the camera glides in toward him in one long soft push, still upside down and still level, arcing round to his front, the lens lengthening from wide toward 35 mm. His face grows in frame: eyes clear and steady, lips relaxed, a slow, even breath, a calm, confident, almost serene look, the suspended instant of a man at ease in free fall; at most the corner of his mouth lifts very slightly at the end.
11.6 to 13.1 seconds: a held close-up of his head and shoulders, the camera drifting only with the fall, his eyes looking straight into the lens, calm, sure and unafraid, the upside-down towers and open sky wheeling slowly behind his head.
Type of shot: a vertigo free-fall shot with a camera dive, a half roll and a slow-motion push-in, in the tradition of the great falling shots. Motion and blur: during the fall the facade, the street and the glass all streak with heavy, directional motion blur and the picture shakes with speed; at the upside-down frame the blur vanishes and everything is crisp, every pane of glass sharp. Lens and focus: a 28 mm wide lens for the fall and ledge, lengthening to about 35 mm as it pushes in, deep focus on the towers until the close-up, where only his face is sharp and the city behind softens into glowing bokeh. Light: hard golden sun and deep blue shade slicing across the towers, his face lit gold on one side and cool blue on the other, sun flares in the glass, a teal-and-orange cinema grade with rich contrast. Mood and feel: dizzying, breathless and awestruck, but cool. Emotion: calm confidence and controlled exhilaration, played small and real, never fear, never panic, never crying and never theatrical, ending in still, sure composure. It must look like a frame from a big-budget thriller shot on film, not a video game, not CGI and not a render.
Every action is performed with natural, realistic human motion: real weight and balance, momentum carried through each movement, and loose, believable arms and shoulders. The written description of the action decides how it looks.
Every person in the video is a real human being with natural skin, hair and clothing. No mannequin, clay figure, capsule, faceless figure or doll appears anywhere, at any moment.
Characters keep their mouths naturally closed; there is no narration, and no subtitles or on-screen text appear.
The sound includes only the roar of wind, a pounding heartbeat and his ragged breath, all dropping low and stretched when the footage slows, and a deep sub-bass hum under the slow motion. There is no speech and there is no music of any kind: no score, no song, no singing and no humming.
