【Camera and Event Script】
One continuous shot with no cuts, in 2.39:1 widescreen, on a motion-control arm carrying a probe lens (a 21 mm lens on a snorkel, aperture T8, so everything stays sharp from a few centimetres to infinity).
Opening, from the first frame to about 0.6 seconds: the camera is 1.2 metres from the hanging parts, level with the middle of the exploded grinder, held dead still, the five parts one above the other in the middle of the frame, the plinth below, a reflector on each side.
The push, from about 0.6 to 3.0 seconds: the camera begins to move in toward the parts, slowly at first (about 23 centimetres closer in 1.8 seconds), then accelerating smoothly up to 1.2 metres a second, the parts growing until their faces fill the frame.
The fly-through: at about 3.4 seconds the camera crosses the axis of the grinder horizontally through the 10-centimetre gap between the burr carrier and the motor housing, 4.6 centimetres from each of the two facing faces, which streak past the lens above and below with strong motion blur; for a moment the frame is only the pale studio.
The whip: from about 3.65 to 3.92 seconds, as it brakes, the camera whips round 180 degrees in three quarters of a second to look back through the gap, all the parts blurring across the frame.
The assembly: from about 4.4 seconds the camera swings out in a 67-degree arc to 60 centimetres from the grinder while the five parts drop one after another, the bottom one first, 0.18 seconds apart, from about 4.75 to 5.47 seconds. Each part falls and closes onto the part below in 0.42 seconds and stops dead with a hard, exact seat and no bounce; the last part, the lid, seats at about 5.5 seconds and the grinder is whole.
The pack shot: from about 5.9 seconds the camera is completely still, 12 degrees above the grinder, framing it on the plinth from the front, centred and fully visible with the plinth and floor beneath it, and holds absolutely still until the end at 8 seconds.
