【Camera and Event Script】
One continuous shot with no cuts, on a 28 mm anamorphic lens, 7 seconds, the camera about one and a half metres from him at chest height, so close that his head and shoulders fill most of the frame and the tank and skyline sit behind him.
The camera stays in the same place and rolls clockwise about the lens axis, a full 360 degrees, with Alex kept in the middle of the frame the whole time. The roll starts at once, slow at first: the horizon is tilted 45 degrees by about 1 second, vertical at 90 degrees by about 1.8 seconds, completely upside down by about 3 seconds with the city lights hanging above him and the roof overhead, past 270 degrees by about 4.3 seconds, and it eases to a smooth stop with the horizon level again at about 5 seconds; it then holds level and still for the last two seconds.
Alex does not fall or move across the frame: he keeps his feet planted on the roof as the world turns, grips the parapet with his left hand, lets his head tip with the horizon, and by about 3 seconds breathes out a short, shaky, breathless laugh without words; at the end he lifts his chin and looks straight into the lens, steadied.
A loose sheet of tarpaulin on the roof billows and snaps in the wind the whole time, and wet roof puddles ripple under blue light.
Every action is performed with natural, realistic human motion: real weight and balance, momentum carried through each movement, and loose, believable arms and shoulders. The written description of the action decides how it looks.
Alex is a real human being with natural skin, hair and clothing. No mannequin, clay figure, capsule, faceless figure or doll appears anywhere, at any moment.
Alex is part of the frame and not a figure placed on top of it. He is lit only by this scene's own light: the same direction, colour and hardness as everything around him, with the same colour cast on his skin and clothes, deep shadow on his shadow side that matches the shadows of the set, and a rim of the scene's light from behind. He is in the same air as the set: the haze, dust, mist or rain of the place falls between the lens and him, softens him a little more as he gets further from the lens, settles on his hair, shoulders and clothes, and is stirred by his movement. His edges are soft and slightly bloomed by the lens like every other edge in the frame, his sharpness and depth of field follow the lens and his distance like the rest of the image, and the film grain is the same on him as on the set. He is firmly in contact with the ground: his feet press into it, he casts a soft shadow on it that matches the other shadows, and the ground, dust and puddles around his feet react to his weight and steps. Wind, draughts and his own motion move his hair and clothes the way they move everything else in the place. His scale against the buildings, furniture and doors is exactly what the blockout shows.
Alex keeps his mouth naturally closed and says nothing; there is no narration, and no subtitles or on-screen text appear.
