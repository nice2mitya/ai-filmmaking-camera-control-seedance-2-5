Pill Gallery

Plan a camera move in simple 3D, then let an AI video model turn it into a real shot. Every person is a striped pill, so the model gets where everyone stands and how the camera moves, with no body to copy.

Previs = a rough 3D plan of a shot, made before the real thing.

1Pick a move below and download its pill clip, the video on the left.
2Send it to a video model with a photo of your own person or product, and the prompt on the card.
3Change the words in the prompt. Keep the sentences that say what the pills are.
How it is made, for the curious

Everything on the left of a card is rendered, not filmed. Each move is a small file that describes a set, some people and a camera, and a renderer plays it back frame by frame.

  1. A 3D scene. The file builds a grey set, places the people and products, and sets the camera: lens, focus and the move. A frame is a pure function of time, so it always comes out the same.
  2. Rendered in a browser. The scene is drawn with three.js inside Remotion, frame by frame in headless Chrome, at 24 frames a second with a film-style shutter, in a wide 2.39:1 frame.
  3. A lens, not a game look. Every frame passes through one chain: soft shadows for the clay look, depth of field worked out from the lens and focus distance, bloom, motion blur, a film tone curve and grain.
  4. People become pills. The animated mannequin (a free CC0 rig with about 80 captured moves, sampled so feet never slide) is swapped for a striped capsule fitted to the body. Products get the same pill. Crates, debris and cars fall the same way every time because their physics is worked out once with Rapier.
  5. Other looks. The same scene can be drawn as an ink storyboard, pencil on the figures, soft ghosts, or an empty plate.
  6. Measured, not typed. Every camera number on a card comes from the camera that renders the frame, and a check catches feet that slide and bodies that pass through each other.
  7. Then the video model. The clip goes in as the reference video, with a photo and a prompt that says what everything really is. It returns a 480p draft; a 1080p final is only made from a draft you approve.

The renderer's source is not in this folder. The clips, the notes and the prompts are everything you need to use the moves.
